Reconstruction and the Intersection Number of the Undirected de Bruijn Graph

Hudson Miller, Colgate University Department of Mathematics. Rutgers Graduate Seminar in Combinatorics, October 14, 2026.

Reconstruction

For a graph G:

A card. G with one vertex deleted.

The deck. The vertex-deletion deck of G, D(G), is the multiset of all cards.

The Kelly–Ulam Conjecture. Every finite simple graph on ≥ 3 vertices is determined up to isomorphism by its vertex-deletion deck.

Equivalently: D(G′) ≅ D(G) ⇒ G′ ≅ G.

Proven for many families: trees, regular graphs, disconnected graphs, maximal planar graphs, and others.

B̂(k, n) does not belong to any of these classes.

De Bruijn sequences

Fix a k-alphabet Σk = {0, 1, …, k − 1} and a length n.

De Bruijn sequence. A cyclic string of length kn in which every word of length n over Σk appears exactly once as a substring, including wrap-around substrings.

Example: k = 2, n = 3. Sequence: 00010111

The undirected de Bruijn graph, B̂(k, n)

De Bruijn graph B(k, n). Vertices: all words of length n − 1 (|V| = kn−1). Edges: there is a directed edge u → v if suf(u) = pre(v).

To “undirect” B(k, n):

  1. Remove the orientation of all edges.
  2. Delete loops.
  3. Collapse all parallel edges.

Adjacency in B̂(k, n). For u ≠ v: u ∼ v if and only if suf(u) = pre(v) or suf(v) = pre(u).

Results

Theorem 1 (Reconstruction). For all k, n ≥ 2, the undirected de Bruijn graph B̂(k, n) on ≥ 3 vertices is reconstructible.

Intersection number i(G) (Erdős–Goodman–Pósa, 1966). The smallest number of cliques needed to cover every edge of G. Computing i(G) is NP-hard in general (Kou–Stockmeyer–Wong, 1978).

Theorem 2 (Intersection Number). For k ≥ 3, n ≥ 4, the intersection number of the undirected de Bruijn graph B̂(k, n) is i(B̂(k, n)) = kn − k(k + 1)/2 − ⅔ k(k − 1)(k + 4).
